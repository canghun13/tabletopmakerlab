Calculation method

Each initial die starts an independent chain. Keep every face value in the total. Rolling the single entered trigger face adds another roll of the same die, which can trigger again. A finite explosion limit is the maximum number of extra rolls per initial die, not a limit for the whole pool.

For a die with s sides, the trigger chance is q = 1/s and the mean face value is (s + 1)/2. Expected total equals initial dice × mean face × (1 + q + q² + … + qᴸ) for finite limit L, or initial dice × mean face / (1 − q) for unlimited chains. Expected extra rolls omit the initial 1 term. A one-sided die with a finite limit is deterministic: each initial die contributes L + 1.

Assumptions and limits

This is a tabletop design scenario using your inputs, not a prediction of player experience. Check your game's exact rules and edge cases before finalizing a design. It does not model penetrating explosions, multiple trigger faces or keep-highest pools.

Supported whole-number inputs are 1–1,000 sides, 1–100 initial dice, a trigger between 1 and the number of sides, and 0–100 extra rolls (0 means unlimited). Target total must be a non-negative safe integer. These are computational limits, not recommended game-design ranges. A one-sided die cannot use an unlimited chain because it never stops.

Target chance uses 30,000 independent trials of the whole pool. Unlimited chains are capped at 80 rolls per initial die in the simulation only. Results vary between runs and a displayed 0.00% is not proof of impossibility for a rare event. Expected values do not use that simulation cap. Finite one-sided chains are evaluated exactly.

Distinguish expected value from chain chance

The default single d6 exploding on 6 has an expected total of 4.2 and 0.2 expected extra rolls. Its exact chance of reaching at least 10 is 1/12 (about 8.33%), so the simulation should be near that value, not identical every time. The 16.67% initial explosion chance describes a different event.

Use the result in a design decision

Test a low target and a high target separately. Explosions can make rare dramatic outcomes available without making ordinary success reliable, which is often the design choice that matters.
