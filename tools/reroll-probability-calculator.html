Calculation method

A fair die has numbered faces from 1 through its side count. On the first roll, a face at or above the threshold succeeds. If that chance is p, one reroll of failures and keeping the best of two both give p × (2 − p); keeping the worst gives p². With “Retry failures for one winning face,” a failed die is retried once and only a single preselected face counts on the retry: p + (1 − p) / sides. The normal threshold does not apply to that retry. Treat each die independently and use the binomial probability of at least the required count, before and after.

Assumptions and limits

This is a tabletop design scenario using your inputs, not a prediction of player experience. Use non-negative safe whole numbers, at least one side per die and 0–1,000 dice. The pool cap is a browser work limit. Zero dice succeed only when zero successes are required; a required count above the pool is a valid 0% event. The one-winning-face retry can produce successes even when the threshold makes first-roll success impossible. Invalid inputs clear the report until corrected. This model does not cover weighted dice, shared reroll budgets or correlated results.

Define exactly what is rerolled

Compare the same success-count event on both sides. With four d6, a threshold of 5 and at least one success required, the baseline is 80.25%. Keeping the worse of two gives 37.57%, a decrease of 42.68 percentage points. Relative change divides the unrounded change by the unrounded baseline. It is unavailable at a zero computed baseline and is not restricted to 0–100%. Probabilities are rounded for display.

Use the result in a design decision

Compare one reroll with an extra die at the same target. They can feel different even when their success chances are similar because rerolls change a failed moment while extra dice change the initial pool.
