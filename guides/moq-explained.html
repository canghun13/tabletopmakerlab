MOQ Explained for Board Game Manufacturing

An MOQ is a supplier constraint. The useful question is whether that run can be funded, stored, sold, and reordered safely for this game.

What MOQ actually means

Minimum order quantity can arise from print setup, component sourcing, die cutting, assembly labor, freight consolidation, or a supplier’s commercial policy. It does not tell you the “right” print run. Ask whether it applies to the complete game, a particular component, or a quoted configuration; a card deck, custom insert, and finished game can each have a different threshold.

Turn the quote into a decision

Put the MOQ beside four project facts: cash required before delivery, realistic sales velocity, storage capacity, and the lead time for a reprint. A 2,000-copy minimum may be manageable for a campaign with committed backers but risky for a first retail run if the remaining stock has no distribution or direct-sales plan.

Practical example

Suppose a factory will make 1,500 copies but charges less per game at 3,000. The lower unit cost is not automatically better. Compare total landed cash, the extra months of inventory, and the reorder point at each quantity. If the 3,000-copy option consumes cash needed for freight, replacements, or marketing, its quoted unit saving may create a larger operating risk.

Questions for the supplier

  • Which specification drives the minimum: the finished game, a custom component, or an assembly step?
  • Can a standard component, shared die, or different packing configuration change it?
  • Is the MOQ a hard minimum, a price break, or a recommended quantity?
  • What lead time and payment schedule apply at each quoted quantity?

Common mistakes

Do not compare unit manufacturing cost alone, and do not treat an MOQ as demand evidence. Keep manufacturing, freight, fulfillment, and storage separate. A crowdfunding campaign’s backer count is also not automatically the quantity to produce: review copies, defect reserve, customer-service replacements, and post-campaign stock need an explicit plan.

Use the numbers together

Start with the quote total in Manufacturer Quote Comparison, then test production reserve with Production Overage and runout timing with Inventory Runway. Record the exact specification and quote date with each scenario so a later change in cards, box, or freight is visible.

Limits

MOQ policies vary by supplier, region, material, and schedule. This guide does not state a universal factory minimum or replace an individual quote.
